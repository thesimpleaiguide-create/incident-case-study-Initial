The Pre-2025 Reality: Five Practice Gaps

NHS Primary Care Standards (Dec 2025)
Operating Environment

Prior to the December 2025 guidance, chaperoning across general practice was largely informal, inconsistent, and predominantly reactive.

The Core Problem

Clinicians relied on patient requests, accepted informal family members, and conducted remote consultations without structured safeguards or defensible records.

The Clinical & Legal Exposure

These systemic habits left patients unprotected, clinicians defenseless against uncorroborated allegations, and practices failing CQC compliance.

1

Reactive, Not Proactive

Inspect

Practice Defect: Waiting for patient requests.

A chaperone was made available on request but rarely offered proactively. Patients unaware of their rights routinely proceeded without safeguards.

2

Missing at Booking

Inspect

Practice Defect: Delayed until examination.

No expectation existed to raise chaperoning during initial booking. Patients arrived unprepared and decisions were rushed in the consultation room.

3

Informal Relatives Accepted

Inspect

Practice Defect: Conflating family with chaperones.

Family members accompanied patients and were casually treated as chaperones despite lacking formal safeguarding training, impartiality, or legal standing.

4

Digital Care Blindspots

Inspect

Practice Defect: Remote consultations excluded.

Policies applied solely to in-person visits. Video examinations and asynchronous digital photo reviews operated without structured chaperone protocols.

5

Poor & Inconsistent Documentation

Inspect

Practice Defect: Indefensible electronic records.

Documentation varied widely across practices, often noting only physical presence while failing to record the offer, patient decision, or staff role.
